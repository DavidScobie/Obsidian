- Body has many hydrogen atoms. With one proton which spins around. This is a moving electrical charge, which makes an electric current. This induces a magnetic field. 
- If we exert an external magnetic field, then the proton spins align either with or against it (men can stand on their feet or their hands). Slightly more stand on their feet (as it is a lower energy level).
- The proton spins dont remain stationary, they precess around in a circle (like a spinning top wobbling if you knock it)
- The precession frequency is the Larmor frequency.    w0 = (gamma)(B0)
- The addition of the magnetic spins creates a net magnetic vector in the z direction. (Longitudinal magnetization). We cannot measure this, as it is in the same direction as the external magnetic field.
- We then send in an RF pulse. This must have the same frequency as the precessing protons. 
- The protons pick up this energy (called resonance). This causes some of the protons to excite to a higher energy level (walk on hands instead of feet). This decreases the net magnetization.
- This causes protons to move in sync (whipping bears), hence they have a net transverse component.
- Viewed from the transverse plane, the precessing net magnetic field induces an electric current in a receiver coil. This is the MRI signal.
**Spatial encoding**
- We alter the magnetic field across the patient. Hence the precession frequency alters too. Hence from the IFT we can deduce where in the body the signal came from.
- We have a slice encoding gradient, frequency encoding gradient and phase encoding gradient.
