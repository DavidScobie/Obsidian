#book 
Author - JJ Sutherland (former reconnaissance pilot in the Vietnam war)

Chapter 1 - The FBI needed to implement a new system, as it's new one involved too much paperwork and approval, resulting in 9/11. They tried the classic [[waterfall]] method based on a [[gantt]] chart, but this was costly and failed. [[Operation_sentinel]](scrum method) worked excellently. 
Takeaways: 'Plans are worthless, but planning is everything'. 'Inspect and adapt'. 'Change or die'. 'Fail fast so you can fix early'.

Chapter 2 - The origins of scrum.
Scrum was inspired by a Toyota manager called 'Deming' in the mid-20th century. He trained people on [[statistical_process_control]] (also called '[[lean]]'). He used a [[PDCA]] method: (example)
Plan - plan how to build the aeroplanes (1 minute). 
Do - build and test as many aeroplanes as possible (3 minutes). 
Check - Look for how the aeroplane building process can be improved. 
Act - Change the way of working.
Takeaways: 'Hesitation is death (observe, orient, decide, act)', 'Look outward for answers', 'great teams are cross-functional, autonomous, empowered', 'PDCA', 'Shu Ha Ri'.

Chapter 3 - Teams
Team productivity varies far more greatly than productivity of individuals.
Great teams are: [[Transcendent]], [[autonomous]], [[cross-functional]].
AI chess story - AI will replace half of jobs by 2034. Although 'humans + machines + process' is stronger than 'machine' alone. Scrum is that process.
Marching story - Be transparent reporting issues in the team, even to seniors. This can achieve [[transcendence]].
Egypt journalism story - at each meeting be sure to ask: what have you been doing? What will you do next? What is getting in the way? (And remove the obstacles) allowing [[autonomy]].
Murder mystery activity, and ant story - Teams with [[cross-functionality]] (AKA diverse teams) perform better than the non-diverse ones, although it can feel more uncomfortable.
Al Qaeda story - Very effective cross-functional team which: surveilled, pinpointed, attacked... to stop terrorists.
Keep teams small: 7+-2 members is best (if too big you'll waste time training newbies + confusion with many conversations).
Electrocution story - Don't blame people for mistakes, it is their situation that is the cause, so change the system. Under poor management, we are all capable of torturing others (out of obedience to authority).

Chapter 4 - Time
Daily scrum meetings (at same O'clock) are very important: 15 mins max, everyone asked: what they did? What they'll do? What's hindering them? Discussion and helping between teams is crucial to keep tasks on track or kill them quickly if necessary.
No labelled roles, anyone can do any type of task.
