RF antennas convert electrical signals into radio waves for transmission and vice versa for reception


![[antenna_diag_1.png]] 
- Here the charged particles move towards each other, cross over, go away from each other, and come back. Like a sine wave. 
- The acceleration of these charged particles warps the magnetic field lines (already familiar for a dipole magnet)

The wave spreads perpendicularly. And as these waves spread further apart, the intensity is shown by the orange line below: This is an EM wave.
![[antenna_img_2.png]]
The wavelength of the EM wave produced is exactly double the length of the dipole.

The oscillating charged particle movement can be obtained with this electrical setup:
![[antenna_img_3.png]] Here the charge builds up on 1 side. Then it repels itself and the charge builds up on the other side. Then it repels itself again and comes back. From up to down this is seen as vertically moving charged particles.

This works the other way around as a receiver (not transmitter). The EM wave is incident on the wire and this forces the charged particles to move up and down.
