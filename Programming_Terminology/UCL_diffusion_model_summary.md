Purpose - Challenge models provide extremely controlled patient
conditions allowing great insight into in-vivo viral replication

**Methods**
- Describe the trends in the viral load data from the challenge study
- Evaluate data complexity via model selection of [[phenomenological_models]]. Used [[Akaike_information_Criterion]] as loss function
- Consider [[well-mixed_models]] to explain the complex dynamics seen in the data
- Spatial inhomogeneity (found in other study) is well-modelled by diffusion model
- Diffusion model visualisations show pattern formation, which model the challenge data well. Also good agreement from [[Target_Cell_Limitation]] and [[interferon_time_delay_after_virus]].

**Discussion**
- Found [[diurnal_variation]] in viral load
- Model dimensions align well with actual human dimensions, and viscosity value
- Small changes in patient structure lead to large differences in patient outcome (3 regimes: spreading infection; oscillatory regime, localized viral peak)

**Limitation**s
- Didn't fit diffusion model to data
- We could involve cell-cell transmission
